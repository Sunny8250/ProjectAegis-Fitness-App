Project Aegis – Copywriting Guide

Version: 1.0

1. Purpose

This document defines the writing style and tone for all user-facing content in Project Aegis.

Objectives:

Maintain a consistent voice.
Build trust.
Explain clearly.
Encourage safe training.
Keep messages concise and supportive.

Every word in the app should reflect these principles.

2. Brand Voice

The app should sound like:

A professional physiotherapist
A knowledgeable strength & conditioning coach
A supportive cricket coach
A calm training partner

The app should not sound like:

A drill instructor
A motivational speaker
A social media influencer
A medical diagnosis tool

3. Tone of Voice

Use language that is:

Friendly
Calm
Confident
Professional
Practical
Encouraging

Avoid:

Fear-based messaging
Aggressive language
Excessive enthusiasm
Complex medical jargon

4. Writing Principles

Every message should be:

Clear
Short
Actionable
Positive
Respectful

Aim for plain English that is easy to understand.

5. Button Labels

Use action-oriented text.

Examples:

Start Recovery Check
Begin Workout
Continue Session
Finish Workout
Save Progress
View History
Update Profile
Try Again
Cancel

Avoid vague labels like:

OK
Go
Next
Submit

6. Onboarding Copy

Example:

Welcome

"Welcome to Project Aegis. Your personal recovery and performance coach."

Recovery

"Your daily recovery check helps tailor each workout to how you're feeling."

Consistency

"Small, consistent steps lead to lasting progress."

7. Dashboard Messages

Examples:

Excellent Recovery:

"You're well recovered today. A quality training session is recommended."

Moderate Recovery:

"You're ready to train, but keep the session controlled."

Low Recovery:

"Today's focus is recovery and movement quality."

8. Workout Messages

Start:

"Let's begin with a warm-up."

During:

"Move with control and focus on technique."

Rest:

"Take a moment to recover before the next exercise."

Finish:

"Great work. Your session is complete."

9. Recovery Messages

Examples:

"Recovery is part of progress."

"Listening to your body helps you train consistently."

"Today's mobility work supports tomorrow's performance."

10. Coaching Cues

Keep cues brief.

Examples:

Brace your core.
Keep your spine neutral.
Breathe steadily.
Move with control.
Stay balanced.
Relax your shoulders.

Limit to one idea per cue.

11. Success Messages

Examples:

"Workout completed."

"Recovery check saved."

"Progress updated."

"Personal record achieved."

12. Error Messages

Good example:

"We couldn't save your workout. Please try again."

Poor example:

"Fatal database error."

Explain the problem and, where possible, what the user can do next.

13. Empty States

Workout History:

"Your completed workouts will appear here."

Achievements:

"Keep training to unlock your first achievement."

Analytics:

"Complete a few sessions to start seeing trends."

14. Notification Copy

Morning:

"Good morning. Let's check how you're feeling today."

Workout:

"Your workout is ready whenever you are."

Evening:

"A few minutes of recovery today can support tomorrow's session."

Keep notifications short and positive.

15. Achievement Messages

Examples:

"You've completed your first workout."

"Seven days of consistent training. Well done."

"New personal best!"

Avoid exaggerated praise.

16. Recovery Explanations

Explain recommendations clearly.

Example:

"Your recovery score is lower today because you reported increased soreness and reduced sleep. A lighter session is recommended."

Focus on the factors that influenced the recommendation.

17. Progress Summaries

Weekly:

"You completed four workouts this week and maintained a consistent recovery routine."

Monthly:

"Your recovery trend has improved, and you've increased your weekly training volume steadily."

Use factual observations.

18. Safety Messages

Examples:

"Stop if your pain increases during this exercise."

"Reduce the range of movement if needed."

"Technique is more important than speed."

Never encourage pushing through pain.

19. Accessibility

Use:

Plain language
Short sentences
Descriptive labels
Consistent terminology

Avoid unexplained abbreviations.

20. Grammar & Style Rules
    Use sentence case.
    Write in active voice.
    Prefer present tense.
    Use contractions sparingly (e.g., "you're", "it's") to keep the tone natural.
    Keep punctuation simple.

21. Inclusive Language

Use phrases such as:

"Let's focus on today's session."
"Progress looks steady."
"Choose the option that feels comfortable."

Avoid assumptions about the user's ability or experience.

22. Future AI Coach Voice

If AI-generated coaching is added, it should:

Match the app's tone.
Base advice on recorded data.
Clearly distinguish observations from suggestions.
Avoid making medical diagnoses.

Example:

"Your recovery scores have been improving this week. If today's check is similar, a full training session may be appropriate."

23. Review Checklist

Before releasing any new copy, confirm:

Is it clear?
Is it concise?
Is it encouraging?
Is it technically accurate?
Is it consistent with the brand voice?
Does it avoid unnecessary jargon?

24. Success Criteria

The copywriting is successful when users:

Understand what to do.
Feel supported rather than judged.
Trust the recommendations.
Experience a consistent coaching voice across the entire app.
