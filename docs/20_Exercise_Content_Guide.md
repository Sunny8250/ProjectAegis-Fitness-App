Project Aegis – Exercise Content Guide

Version: 1.0

1. Purpose

This document defines how every exercise should be written, displayed, and coached within Project Aegis.

Objectives:

Maintain consistent coaching quality.
Improve exercise technique.
Reduce injury risk.
Support rehabilitation and cricket performance.
Standardise all exercise content.

Every exercise in the app must follow this guide.

3. Naming Rules

Exercise names should:

Be widely recognised.
Be short and descriptive.
Avoid unnecessary abbreviations.
Use consistent terminology.

Examples:

Bird Dog
Dead Bug
Glute Bridge
Side Plank
Chin Tuck

7. Exercise Purpose

Each exercise should explain:

Why it exists.
Which movement it improves.
Which training goal it supports.

Example:

Bird Dog

Purpose:

Improve core stability while maintaining a neutral spine.

8. Target Muscles

Identify:

Primary muscles

Secondary muscles

Example:

Primary:

Core
Glutes

Secondary:

Shoulders
Lower Back

9. Benefits

List 3–5 practical benefits.

Example:

Improves spinal stability.
Enhances balance and coordination.
Supports efficient bowling mechanics.
Reduces unnecessary trunk movement.

Focus on realistic outcomes rather than exaggerated claims.

10. When to Use

Specify ideal situations.

Example:

Bird Dog

Use:

During warm-up
Core sessions
Recovery days
Lower back rehabilitation
Bowling preparation

11. Contraindications

State when the exercise should be avoided or modified.

Example:

Avoid if:

Sharp pain during movement.
Acute wrist injury without modification.
Symptoms worsen during the exercise.

If pain persists or is severe, the app should advise the user to stop the exercise and consider seeking guidance from a qualified healthcare professional.

12. Setup Instructions

Keep setup simple.

Example:

Start on hands and knees.
Place hands under shoulders.
Place knees under hips.
Maintain a neutral spine.

Maximum:

Five setup steps.

13. Step-by-Step Instructions

Use numbered instructions.

Example:

Extend one arm forward.
Extend the opposite leg backward.
Keep hips level.
Pause briefly.
Return slowly.
Repeat on the opposite side.

14. Breathing

Every exercise must include breathing instructions.

Example:

Breathe in before the movement.

Breathe out as you reach or exert effort.

Never encourage holding the breath unless specifically appropriate for advanced techniques.

15. Tempo

Specify movement speed.

Example:

2–1–2

2 sec up

1 sec hold

2 sec down

Mobility exercises may instead specify slow, controlled movement rather than strict timing.

16. Coaching Cues

Limit to 3–5 concise cues.

Example:

Keep your spine neutral.
Move slowly.
Brace your core gently.
Keep your hips level.

Use simple language.

17. Common Mistakes

Examples:

Arching the lower back.
Holding the breath.
Rushing the movement.
Shrugging the shoulders.
Losing balance.

Provide corrections where appropriate.

18. Progressions

Progressions should increase difficulty gradually.

Example:

Dead Bug

↓

Bird Dog

↓

Side Plank

↓

Single-Leg Bird Dog

Only one progression step should be introduced at a time.

19. Regressions

Provide an easier alternative.

Example:

Push-up

↓

Incline Push-up

↓

Wall Push-up

The regression should target the same movement pattern.

20. Cricket Relevance

Every exercise should explain its relevance to cricket where applicable.

Example:

Bird Dog:

Improves trunk stability during bowling.
Helps transfer force from the lower body to the upper body.
Supports balance during the delivery stride.

If there is no specific cricket benefit, state that the exercise supports general athletic development.

21. Safety Notes

Include brief reminders such as:

Stop if pain increases.
Maintain controlled movement.
Prioritise technique over speed.
Reduce the range of motion if advised by the programme.

22. Video Standards

Each exercise video should:

Be 20–60 seconds long.
Show the full body where possible.
Demonstrate from multiple angles if needed.
Use good lighting.
Avoid distracting backgrounds.

23. Voice Script (Future)

Future voice guidance should include:

Exercise name.
Setup.
Key coaching cues.
Breathing reminders.
Encouragement.
Completion cue.

Example:

"Start in a hands-and-knees position. Keep your spine neutral and extend the opposite arm and leg. Move slowly and keep your hips level."

24. Quality Checklist

Every exercise entry should answer:

Is the purpose clear?
Are the instructions easy to follow?
Are breathing cues included?
Are safety notes present?
Are progression and regression options defined?
Is the cricket relevance explained where appropriate?
